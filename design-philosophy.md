# Corrective Memory

## A Design Philosophy for RL Correction MCP

### The Movement

**Corrective Memory** is an aesthetic movement born from the tension between error and refinement. It treats the act of correction not as failure but as the most elegant form of learning — a palimpsest where wrong outputs are not erased but preserved alongside their corrected counterparts, creating layered meaning through juxtaposition.

### Space and Form

Space breathes with intentional asymmetry. The canvas is divided not by rigid grids but by organic thresholds — zones of activity separated by generous negative space that suggests the pause between stimulus and response. Forms emerge as soft-edged geometric fragments: rounded rectangles that feel like memory blocks, circular nodes that pulse like synaptic connections, thin connective lines that trace the invisible paths of retrieval. Nothing is perfectly aligned; everything is precisely placed. The composition should feel like a diagram of an elegant mind at work — systematic yet organic, analytical yet warm.

### Color and Material

The palette draws from the Anthropic design system but deepens it into something more contemplative. Terracotta (#c96442) serves as the anchoring warmth — the color of correction, of human intervention. Coral (#d97757) provides the glow of refinement in progress. A muted steel blue (#6a9bcc) represents the cold precision of retrieval algorithms. Olive green (#788c5d) signifies growth through iteration. The background is parchment-warm (#faf9f5), like a well-worn notebook where corrections have been made and remade. Colors are applied with the restraint of a master watercolorist — each hue earning its place through deliberate, sparing application.

### Scale and Rhythm

The rhythm follows a correction cycle: sparse → dense → sparse. Large open zones give way to clusters of concentrated visual information — like retrieval results densely packed with meaning — then breathe again into quiet space. Scale variation is dramatic but controlled: monumental typography for the project name anchors the composition, while microscopic labels and reference markers create a rewarding second read. The eye moves in a Z-pattern, discovering new details with each pass, as though the image itself learns through repeated viewing.

### Composition and Balance

Every element is placed with the painstaking attention of a master craftsman who has spent countless hours refining each position. The composition achieves balance through tension, not symmetry — a heavy terracotta block on one side is counterweighted by a constellation of small coral nodes on the other. Typography is sparse and essential: the project name rendered large as a declaration, a single descriptive phrase floating in space, systematic reference markers that suggest this is a diagram from an imaginary discipline. Text never explains; it anchors. The overall composition should feel like an artifact that proves something ephemeral — learning, memory, correction — can be studied, mapped, and understood through careful visual attention.

### Visual Hierarchy

The hierarchy reads like a scientific observation: primary identity (project name, monumental), secondary context (descriptor, whispered), tertiary detail (reference markers, barely there). Each level is separated by generous breathing room. The result must appear as though someone at the absolute top of their field labored over every detail with painstaking care — an object that could be displayed in a museum of computational aesthetics, where the beauty of machine learning correction is given the reverence typically reserved for fine art. The final work should feel meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise, every alignment and color choice the result of countless refinements.
